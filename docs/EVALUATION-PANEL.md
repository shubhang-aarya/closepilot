## The tradeoff, stated rather than hidden

**ATTEST does not win everything, and the row that proves it is `exact_only`.**

`exact_only` has a **false-proof rate of 0.0%** against ATTEST's 4.8%. It is the
most precise method on this panel and ATTEST is not. It achieves that by only
ever answering when a UTR or an order reference matches exactly — 22 settlements
of 500, and it declines the other 478.

That is the whole argument. Precision alone is trivially maximised by refusing
to answer. `exact_only` is a system that is never wrong and rarely useful.

At the other end, **`greedy` decides 462 of 500** — it almost always has an
answer — and **95.0% of those answers are wrong**. A system that always answers
and is usually wrong is worse than no system, because it produces confident
journal entries.

`fuzzy` sits between and gets the worst of both: 3.6% coverage and a 40%
false-proof rate.

ATTEST resolves **16.0%** — three and a half times `exact_only` — at a
false-proof rate of 4.8%, and every one of those answers carries a certificate
that an independent kernel re-derived from source records. **The policy layer
then refuses to post most of them anyway**: 1 auto-post of 250 in the canonical
run, because expected loss priced against the cost of a human check is the
second gate after proof.

The claim is not "ATTEST is the most accurate." It is:

> Maximise useful automation subject to a hard refusal to assert unsupported
> proofs — and make the refusals as legible as the answers.

## Where this is honest about itself

`exact_only`'s superior precision is published in the product, on the Trust
lens, as claim **C-004: "ATTEST is not the most precise method on this panel."**
It is not buried in an appendix. A system that only reports the comparisons it
wins has not been evaluated.

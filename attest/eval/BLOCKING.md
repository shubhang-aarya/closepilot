## Three numbers are all called "blocking recall"

| name | what it measures | why it differs |
|---|---|---|
| `STATIC` | every settlement scored at the ladder's **terminal** rung, no consumption | the ceiling the ladder *offers* |
| `ORACLE` | same, but the true bundle is consumed after each settlement, in a stated order | isolates what consumption does to **pool size**, with recall held harmless |
| `LIVE` | the pools `pipeline.run` actually used -- a **mixed-rung** set, whatever rung each settlement stopped at | the ceiling the engine *realises* |

This is why `FAILURES.md` quotes a ceiling near 1.0 (1.0000 as measured here) and the live run reads 0.9558: the first is `STATIC` at a uniform terminal rung, the second is `LIVE` over `pools_used`, and almost nothing ever escalates off rung 0. They are different measurements of different things and neither is wrong. Every row below says which it is.

## 3. `CHARGEBACK_REVERSAL` -- expected to fail, and it does, but not for the expected reason

The contract expects this family to defeat blocking because the reversing order reaches 14-30 calendar days back. Located structurally (the loose order whose net exactly closes `sum(bundle) - credit`), all 4 of 4 were found, at business-day lags **12, 17, 18, 24** -- far outside any ladder considered.

**But its blocking recall is 1.0000 at every ladder in the sweep, including `(2)`.** The reversing order is not in the truth set, so it never counts against the ceiling. What it does instead is make the credit *smaller* than the bundle that produced it:

| settlement | reversal lag | credit | sum(bundle) | reversal net |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `setl_000109` | 24 | 631,603 | 682,959 | 51,356 |
| `setl_000121` | 17 | 323,104 | 704,250 | 381,146 |
| `setl_000161` | 18 | 1,360,491 | 1,449,710 | 89,219 |
| `setl_000201` | 12 | 664,492 | 817,120 | 152,628 |

`sum(bundle) > credit` for all four. `subsetsum.solve` searches **positive** subsets summing to the credit, so the true explanation -- bundle *minus* a reversal -- is not in the search space at any window width. Verified directly: at a single rung covering lags 2-24 with `span=40`, pools of 558-823 orders that contain the entire true bundle *and* (in 3 of 4 cases) the reversing order itself, `solve` returns AMBIGUOUS and recovers the truth in **0 of 4**.

## 5. Consumption, and the order it depends on

Consumption makes pools **order-dependent**: the pool a settlement sees depends on which settlements were decided before it. Two orders are reported. *easiest-first* is the engine's own (`pipeline.run` sorts by rung-0 pool size), chosen there because a settlement with a small pool is both likely to be decidable and likely to free orders for everyone else. *chronological* is by `settled_on`, included because consumption and the settlement calendar are correlated and that turns out to matter a great deal.

Consumption here is **oracle**: the true bundle is consumed, not a proven one. True bundles are pairwise disjoint by construction, so oracle consumption cannot remove an order another settlement needs -- which is why the ceiling is identical in all three columns and the entire effect lands on pool size. This is the upper bound on what consumption can buy.

| ladder | p50 none | p50 easiest | p50 chrono | p90 easiest | max easiest | ceiling (all three) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `2` | 45 | 22 | 22 | 70 | 138 | 0.9433 |
| `2 > 4` | 79 | 41 | 25 | 98 | 166 | 1.0000 |
| `2 > 3 > 4` | 107 | 64 | 27 | 138 | 215 | 1.0000 |
| `1 > 2 > 3 > 4 > 5 > 6` | 217 | 108 | 57 | 212 | 286 | 1.0000 |

**Consumption is the largest single lever in this study, and evaluation order is worth more than the ladder.** At the shipped ladder, oracle consumption takes p50 from 107 to 64 easiest-first and to 27 chronologically -- a 4.0x cut, against the 1.4x available from dropping a rung. Chronological wins because a settlement's bundle sits at capture dates immediately before its payout, so consuming in date order frees exactly the dates the next settlement queries; easiest-first scatters consumption across the calendar. This is not a recommendation to change the engine's order -- easiest-first is chosen for cascade decidability, not pool size, and the live measurement below is what would decide it -- but the gap is large enough to be worth someone's attention.

### The finding

At the shipped ladder, **1 of 250 settlements ever resolves past rung 0** (`{'L3-dp/r0': 41, 'L2-single/r0': 10, 'L3-dp/r2': 1}`). Rungs 3 and 4 are very nearly dead code -- and that is not the reassurance it sounds like.

Escalation fires on `CONTRADICTED` -- the signal that the true explanation was pruned. **A pool with the truth pruned does not reliably return `CONTRADICTED`. It can return `PROVEN` of something else.** When it does, the cascade stops, and the wider rungs that held the answer are never reached. Widening a ladder cannot repair a false proof manufactured at rung 0, because the false proof is what suppresses the widening. Rung 0 carries essentially all of the risk.

The `(1)>(2)>(3)>(4)` row is that mechanism at full strength. Rung 0 = lag 1 has a STATIC ceiling of 0.0000 -- no true order is ever in it -- and the engine posts **9 wrong entries out of 25**, pair precision 0.380, LIVE ceiling 0.5998. Every one is internally consistent, kernel-checked and factually wrong. Blocking errors manufacture proofs, and this is what it looks like when they do.

Widening hurts too, in the opposite direction. `(2)>(4)` raises the LIVE ceiling from 0.9558 to 0.9725 and gains no exact matches, but posts a false proof on `setl_000016` (`split_order`) where the truth *was* fully in the pool and no correct answer exists at all -- the credit is half an order short of its bundle. The wider rung-1 window simply supplied enough decoys for a wrong 5-set to become uniquely satisfiable. **Both directions manufacture false proofs: too narrow prunes the truth, too wide supplies a substitute.**

### What actually holds the line, and it is not the ladder

The shipped ladder posts **0** wrong entries. Ablating one thing at a time, ladder held fixed at `(2)>(3)>(4)`:

| | p50 | exact set | WRONG | pair prec. | false proofs |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| as shipped | 49 | 52 | **0** | 1.000 | -- |
| consumption OFF | 52 | 48 | **3** | 0.955 | `chargeback_reversal`; `chargeback_reversal`; `timing_gap` |
| amount filter OFF | 54 | 52 | **0** | 1.000 | -- |

**Global consumption is the false-proof control, not the window.** Turning it off costs 4 correct answers and adds 3 wrong ones at an identical ceiling and an identical ladder. The amount filter changes nothing but pool size.

`setl_000246` shows the mechanism concretely. It is the settlement that was the single WRONG in the D3 run, on this same seed, at this same ladder. Traced through the current engine:

```
  setl_000246   timing_gap   (settles T+4; rung 0 offers T+2 only)
  decided 97th of 250 in easiest-first order, 222 orders already consumed
  rung 0   pool =   0   truth in pool 0/4   -> CONTRADICTED  -> escalate
  rung 1   pool =  40   truth in pool 0/4   -> AMBIGUOUS (4) -> decline
  static rung-0 pool for the same settlement, with no consumption: 26 orders
```

Blocking offered the same 26 candidates it always did, and exactly one wrong subset of them closed within tolerance -- that was the D3 false proof. What changed is that 222 orders had been consumed by earlier proofs before this settlement was reached, leaving the pool **empty**. An empty pool cannot manufacture anything: it returns CONTRADICTED, the cascade escalates, and the engine declines honestly. **The false proof was killed by constraint propagation, not by a wider window** -- which is the same conclusion the ablation above reaches from the other direction.

## 8. Bug found, not patched

`attest/blocking.py` is frozen, so this is reported rather than fixed, per `AGENTS.md`.

`_capture_dates_for(settled_on, lag, span=12)` scans back only `span` calendar days. A lag of L business days spans up to ceil(7L/5)+2 calendar days once weekends fan in, so **the default span answers correctly only up to lag 8**. Above that it silently returns a truncated set, and from lag 11 it returns the empty set.

Reproduction:

```python
>>> from datetime import date
>>> from attest.blocking import _capture_dates_for
>>> [len(_capture_dates_for(date(2026, 6, 15), lag)) for lag in (8, 9, 10, 11)]
>>> [1, 0, 0, 0]        # span=12, the shipped default
>>> [1, 1, 1, 3]        # span=60, correct
```

Nothing shipped is affected -- `LAG_LADDER` tops out at 4 and every lag in the sweep is under 8. The hazard is latent: raising `LAG_LADDER` past 8 adds a rung that quietly does nothing, and it fails by producing an empty pool -> `CONTRADICTED` -> escalate to the next equally empty rung, which reads as "the data has no answer" rather than "the window is broken". Suggested shape if it is ever touched: derive the span from the largest lag in `LAG_LADDER` rather than defaulting it, or assert `max(LAG_LADDER) <= 8`.

## 9. Recommendation

### Keep `LAG_LADDER = (2, 3, 4)`. No change.

Every alternative in this sweep is neutral or worse. The shipped ladder is the only configuration measured that posts the maximum number of correct answers *and* zero false proofs:

| ladder | p50 | exact set | WRONG | pair prec. | LIVE ceiling | verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `(2)>(3)>(4)` | 49 | 52 | **0** | 1.000 | 0.9558 | **shipped -- keep** |
| `(2)` | 45 | 51 | **0** | 1.000 | 0.9433 | -1 exact, lower ceiling |
| `(2)>(4)` | 49 | 52 | **1** | 0.983 | 0.9725 | +1 false proof (does not replicate, S10) |
| `(2+4)` | 77 | 20 | **0** | 1.000 | 1.0000 | -32 exact for no gain |
| `(2+3+4)` | 107 | 13 | **0** | 1.000 | 1.0000 | -39 exact for no gain |
| `(1)>(2)>(3)>(4)` | 54 | 16 | **9** | 0.380 | 0.5998 | **+9 false proofs** |

In the contract's own form, for the change the STATIC table appears to argue for -- dropping the lag-3 rung, which buys zero true pairs:

> **Dropping lag 3 costs 0.0% STATIC ceiling and saves 26.2% of terminal-rung pool size -- and it is still the wrong trade, because the terminal rung is reached by 1 settlement in 250. The realised saving is 2.8% of p90, and the realised cost is one false proof: WRONG 0 -> 1, precision 1.000 -> 0.983.**

The false proof in that sentence is a single event and **it does not replicate at n=1000, where the signs reverse** -- see section 10 before quoting it. What survives both samples is the first clause, and it is the study's main practical warning: **the STATIC sweep and the LIVE run disagree about the value of a rung, and the LIVE run is the one that posts entries.** A rung reached by 1 settlement in 250 cannot be worth 26% of anything. Any future ladder change argued from terminal-rung pool sizes is arguing from a measurement the engine does not take. Dropping lag 3 is not harmful on this evidence; it is simply not *worth* anything, and an unmeasurable change to a frozen file is not a change worth defending in review.

### Tried and rejected: a union rung 0, `LAG_LADDER = ((2, 4),)`

The mechanism in section 6 says rung 0 carries all the false-proof risk, so the structural fix is a rung 0 that already contains every lag the portfolio settles at. It works exactly as predicted -- LIVE ceiling 1.0000, zero false proofs -- and it is still not worth applying:

> **The union rung costs 12.8% exact-set match and +57.1% pool size to buy +0.0442 ceiling and +0 false proofs.**

32 correct answers surrendered for a risk that is already at zero. The bigger pool does not lose the truth -- it drowns it: those settlements do not become wrong, they become AMBIGUOUS, declined count 198 -> 230. Worth recording because the argument for it is sound and the measurement still says no. If a future change re-exposes rung 0 (see below), this is the fix to reach for.

### The residual risk, and the cheap guard for it

Zero false proofs today is a property of two things holding at once: rung-0 STATIC recall of 0.9433, and global consumption emptying the pools that would otherwise be just large enough to close a wrong subset. Neither is asserted anywhere in the code, and the `(1)>...` row shows the cost of losing the first: 9 wrong entries, precision 0.380. Cheapest guard, and the only change this study actually asks for beyond "keep the ladder": treat rung-0 STATIC recall as a tracked metric rather than an emergent one, so a future lag change cannot silently take it to 0.0000. **Not applied -- flagged for a human.**

### Not recommended, and the reason is worth recording

Widening to catch `CHARGEBACK_REVERSAL` (section 3). It costs 9.8x the median pool and buys nothing, because the family is a *sign* problem, not a *window* problem. The contract expected this family to be the case that justifies a wide ladder. It is the case that proves a wide ladder is the wrong instrument.

## 10. Sample size

One wrong entry in 250 is a thin signal, and the honest reading is that this study cannot distinguish a WRONG rate of 0.0% from 0.4% on 250 settlements. The mechanism (section 6) is what carries the recommendation; the count is corroboration.

Re-measured at `n=1000`, same seed:

| ladder | n | p50 | exact set | WRONG | declined | pair prec. | ceiling |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `(2)>(3)>(4)` | 1000 | 155 | 51 (5.1%) | **1** (0.1%) | 948 | 0.947 | 0.9418 |
| `(2)>(4)` | 1000 | 158 | 51 (5.1%) | **0** (0.0%) | 949 | 1.000 | 0.9446 |
| `(2+4)` | 1000 | 324 | 45 (4.5%) | **0** (0.0%) | 955 | 1.000 | 0.9995 |

**The sign flips, and that has to be said plainly.** At n=250 the shipped ladder posts 0 wrong and `(2)>(4)` posts 1; at n=1000 the shipped ladder posts 1 and `(2)>(4)` posts 0. The "dropping lag 3 costs a false proof" result in section 9 does **not** replicate at n=1000. Both measurements are 0-1 events; neither distinguishes the two ladders. The honest reading is that **among ladders whose rung 0 contains lag 2, the WRONG differences in this study are noise**, and the recommendation to keep `(2, 3, 4)` rests on *no alternative demonstrably improving anything*, not on alternatives being demonstrably worse.

What does replicate, in both samples and in the ablation, is the part that matters:

* a rung 0 without lag 2 posts 9 wrong entries at precision 0.380 -- an order of magnitude, not a coin flip;
* disabling global consumption, ladder unchanged, moves WRONG from 0 to 3;
* the union rung reaches a ~1.0 ceiling at both sizes (1.0000 at n=250, 0.9995 at n=1000) and costs exact matches at both (52->20 and 51->45).

Note that exact-set match *falls* with portfolio size (20.8% at n=250, 5.1% at n=1000) while the pool grows: more settlements per capture date means more subsets satisfy the same credit, so uniqueness -- not search -- is the binding constraint. That is the same conclusion `FAILURES.md` reaches on D3 from a different direction, and it is why a blocking change can only ever move this metric a little.
